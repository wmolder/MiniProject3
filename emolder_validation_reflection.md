Across the ten projects in the validation log, long quiet spells did not follow one uniform activity pattern. Declining activity appeared in BlenderProc, cartography, and SFA, while DICOM Utilities and IRAF were described as irregular; other projects were cyclical, steady, or rising. These labels are useful warnings, not forecasts: a declining or irregular trace can precede a pause, but the time series alone does not tell whether maintainers have left, work is seasonal, or a new phase is about to begin.

When activity did return, the log points to concrete events rather than a generic passage of time. In the exoplanet catalogue, volunteer submissions restarted contributions and later automation sustained data updates. A user-reported workflow problem prompted fixes and continued maintenance in the Snakemake project; IRAF’s community takeover and the Precession v2 rewrite marked new phases of work. Intel-QS followed a refactor and build-system changes with a large contribution burst, while PlasmaPy moved from early status and citation updates to substantive code and later growth. These cases suggest that a new maintainer, a solvable user need, a major technical transition, or automation can turn a dormant-looking period into renewed or sustained activity.

The contrast cases clarify when a post-gap commit should not count as recovery. Cartography’s isolated CRAN-compliance release and SFA’s README-only touch-up were not treated as renewed development. At the same time, both were adjudicated Active in the current-status field, as were all projects in this log. “Active now” and “recovered after a gap” answer different questions; a project can still be maintained without showing a substantive recovery event, and a long gap need not mean permanent death if stewardship or development later resumes.

Recurring pitfalls were reading a single commit as a comeback, treating declining or irregular curves as proof of abandonment, and confusing episodic bursts with a steady rise. Automation also needs interpretation: nightly updates demonstrate sustained project activity, but not necessarily renewed human feature development. The adjudications resolve these ambiguities by weighing commit purpose, issue and pull-request context, ownership changes, and what followed the apparent restart—not just the graph shape or commit count. The log records AuthorKept resolutions and post-discussion agreement throughout, while several initial disagreements around recovery, activity pattern, and current status show why those contextual checks mattered.
